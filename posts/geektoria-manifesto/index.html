The Geektoria Manifesto

2025-06-13

Geektoria is an open, specialized, and unapologetically geeky online journal — an ultra-specialized, non-commercial, non-mainstream publishing project designed in direct contrast to traditional content media structures. It is not built for the masses, nor for the market. There is no SEO, no click-optimization, no social media algorithm engineering here.

Geektoria is a multilingual space (currently in Persian, English, Russian, and Arabic) for minds seeking writing that transcends the most superficial layers of the internet. 🧭 The Three Foundational Principles of Geektoria:

  1. No Publishing Structures This journal is not affiliated with any official institution, publisher, or market-driven agenda. Its entire structure is deliberately anti-model and anti-publishing. Geektoria operates outside the conventional frameworks of media and publishing: no publishers, no licenses, no traditional-style editors. Anything that smells like the content industry has no place here.

  2. Content Indecipherable to Language Models Only works deeply embedded with human experience, professional intuition, and unconscious layers will be published — the kind of content that no language model, not even GPT-6, can truly understand by just crawling the internet. At most, maybe two or three people on Earth might have had similar experiences. By content, we mean material so fused with human depth, expert-level intuition, and personal insights that no language model — even GPT-n+ — could possibly reconstruct it from the internet alone.

  3. The Reader is a Network Node, Not a Consumer No one here is just a reader. Anyone who reads or writes is an active node in a living network. To read is to participate in computation. To contribute is to engage in nonlinear interaction — not just typing a paragraph. Connections are designed like a mesh topology, not based on traditional hierarchies. 🧱 Supplementary Principles of Geektoria:

  4. Language is not just a Tool — It’s a Battlefield Writings are never simplified. Each text demands its own mental compiler. Some articles may take months just to be understood — and this is fully intentional. Texts are purposefully complex, layered, and open to interpretation. Each article invents its own linguistic ecosystem. Simplification is taboo.

  5. Code is Part of Our Language You may encounter pseudo-code, shell snippets, config files, or even Git comments within articles. Our language is not limited to human language. Understanding certain concepts requires multilingual expression — including code. The goal here is not readability, but linguistic challenge and multi-layered comprehension.

  6. Every Article is a Mental Seed — Not a Final Product The writings are not designed to deliver answers, but to embed a question in your mind — one that may never be resolved, but whose path of discovery is worth the journey. Articles may remain half-cooked and fermenting for days or months. We don’t write by calendar — we write by mental readiness and thematic ripeness. Here, articles are crafted to plant questions, not deliver solutions. Some of these questions will never be solved — and that unresolved tension in your mind is the real message.
